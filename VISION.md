# Vision

Machina Practica packages should let teams reuse proven engineering mechanics while retaining control of product rules, architecture, privacy promises, and presentation.

A useful shared package removes duplication and maintenance from real consumers. It earns its boundary through working implementations, independent adoption, and retained evidence. A shared intention alone is not enough to justify a library.

Compatibility is a product promise. Consumers should be able to reproduce behavior from their retained histories and artifacts, understand which interfaces are stable, and migrate deliberately. Unknown data and unverifiable artifacts deserve explicit failure rather than guessed recovery.

Packages should remain small and layered, with standards and injected interfaces preferred over hidden infrastructure. No universal application framework, mandatory design system, or forced migration is intended. Existing local implementations remain valid until extraction proves a net benefit.

Success means more reliable systems and less repeated maintenance across independent projects, supported by inspectable evidence.
